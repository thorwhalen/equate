equate.matching.quadratic¶

Quadratic matchers: align the nodes of two graphs (network alignment).

The second matcher arity (decision register D8, doc 12 §4). A flat matcher picks pairs from a fixed score matrix; here how well u matches v *depends on how their neighbors are matched* — a quadratic assignment problem — so the scores and the correspondence are produced together, from the two graphs. Each quadratic matcher builds a node-pair score matrix and hands it to a flat matcher (extract=, default the optimal LAP) for the final 1:1 correspondence.

  • 'isorank' (core) — IsoRank (Singh, Xu & Berger 2008): “two nodes match if their neighbors match”, the stationary point of a random walk on the product graph, blended with an optional prior node_affinity.

  • 'rrwm' (equate[graph-matching]) — pygmtools’ reweighted random walk solver on the full quadratic affinity (edge-weight agreement + node_affinity).

Use align() (also equate.align), or pick a strategy from the quadratic_matchers registry. seeds are known node correspondences (anchors): they are kept as given and steer the rest.

equate.matching.quadratic.align(G1, G2, *, how='isorank', node_affinity=None, seeds=None, **config)[source]¶

Align the nodes of two graphs -> a Matching of node pairs.

how picks the quadratic matcher ('isorank' core, 'rrwm' via equate[graph-matching], or a callable); node_affinity is an optional prior (an (n1, n2) array or a (u, v) -> float callable, non-negative) and usually what makes topology-only alignment work (see isorank_scores()); seeds are known (G1 node, G2 node) anchors. Other keywords configure the matcher (e.g. extract='greedy', alpha=0.9). Graphs may be networkx graphs, {node: neighbors} mappings or adjacency matrices.

>>> star = {'hub': ['x', 'y', 'z'], 'x': ['hub'], 'y': ['hub'], 'z': ['hub']}
>>> other = {0: [3], 1: [3], 2: [3], 3: [0, 1, 2]}
>>> dict(align(star, other).labeled_pairs())['hub']
3
equate.matching.quadratic.isorank(G1, G2, *, node_affinity=None, seeds=None, extract='optimal', label=None, **config)[source]¶

Align two graphs with IsoRank -> a Matching of node pairs.

matching.labeled_pairs() yields (G1 node, G2 node); .plan holds the full IsoRank score matrix. extract is the flat matcher that turns it into a 1:1 correspondence (any name or callable equate.matching.resolve_matcher() takes); **config goes to isorank_scores() (alpha, max_iter, tol).

>>> G1 = {'a': ['b'], 'b': ['a', 'c', 'd'], 'c': ['b'], 'd': ['b', 'e'], 'e': ['d']}
>>> G2 = {1: [2, 4], 2: [1], 3: [4], 4: [1, 3, 5], 5: [4]}  # the same graph, renamed
>>> dict(isorank(G1, G2).labeled_pairs())['b'], dict(isorank(G1, G2).labeled_pairs())['d']
(4, 1)
equate.matching.quadratic.isorank_scores(G1, G2, *, node_affinity=None, seeds=None, alpha=0.8, max_iter=100, tol=1e-09, label=None)[source]¶

The IsoRank node-pair score matrix R (n1 x n2, sums to 1).

Iterates R <- alpha * W1 R W2^T + (1 - alpha) * E to its fixed point, where W is each graph’s column-stochastic walk matrix and E the normalized prior (node_affinity; uniform when None). seeds pin their rows/columns of the prior to the given partner (at the prior’s own top value), so their anchoring propagates to the neighbors. A directed graph walks against its edges (the column normalization uses in-degrees). Warns when max_iter runs out before tol. Returned as a ScoreMatrix (sense='maximize') labeled with the two node lists.

The prior does real work: from topology alone (node_affinity=None) the scores tend to the product of node degrees, which cannot tell equal-degree nodes apart. Pass a node similarity (attributes, names, degrees) for anything but degree-distinct graphs.

equate.matching.quadratic.rrwm(G1, G2, *, node_affinity=None, seeds=None, extract='optimal', label=None, sigma=1.0, **rrwm_params)[source]¶

Align two graphs with pygmtools’ RRWM — requires equate[graph-matching].

Builds the quadratic affinity K from Gaussian edge-weight agreement (bandwidth sigma) with node_affinity on its diagonal, solves it with pygmtools.rrwm (**rrwm_params forwarded), and extracts a 1:1 correspondence with the flat extract matcher. Seeds condition the solve: every assignment contradicting one (its node paired elsewhere) is removed from K and the seed pair gets the top affinity, so the rest is aligned around it; seeds are then kept as given. Graphs with no edges and no prior fall back to a uniform node affinity. Uses the numpy backend and never changes pygmtools’ global backend.
