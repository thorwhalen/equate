equate.sources¶

Align several sources of the same items: one group per item, at most one record per source.

Several sources often describe the same items, each partially and each with its own kind of error, and no shared identifier: a recording split into utterances (good timing, noisy transcript), a printed sheet (exact text, no timing), an OCR or PDF extraction (exact text, broken layout). align_sources() decides which records of the sources are the same item; equate.fusion.fuse() then merges each item field by field and reports where the sources disagree.

The alignment is progressive (the classic multiple-sequence-alignment strategy): the first source is the backbone, and each further source is aligned against the groups built so far. One step is a two-collection matching problem over a gain matrix, gain[g, j] = similarity(group g, record j) - threshold (a record’s similarity to a group is its best similarity to any member), solved by the order strategy:

  • 'monotone' (default): the sources list the items in the same order, gaps allowed. Solved by equate’s needleman_wunsch_alignment() with the gain as substitution score and free gaps, so a record missing from one source becomes a gap instead of shifting everything after it. Records left over on both sides within window positions of each other are then paired by assignment when they match, which is how a local order swap (two items read in the opposite order) is recovered rather than cascading; such groups are marked reordered.

  • 'free': no order; an optimal assignment (equate’s 'assign' matcher). Any other matcher name from equate.matchers ('greedy', 'stable') works the same way.

  • any callable gain ScoreMatrix -> iterable of (g, j): your own strategy.

Records are compared on the fields they share (compare), with equate’s comparators or any callable, so domain knowledge (a phonetic reading, a unit normaliser) is injected, not built in. A group’s margin is its score minus the best score a nearby rival record would get in its place: low (or negative) where the alignment is unsure.

>>> sheet = [{'text': 'bonjour'}, {'text': 'merci'}, {'text': 'au revoir'}]
>>> audio = [{'text': 'bonjou', 'start': 0.0}, {'text': 'au revoar', 'start': 2.1}]
>>> al = align_sources({'sheet': sheet, 'audio': audio})
>>> [g.members for g in al]
[{'sheet': 0, 'audio': 0}, {'sheet': 1}, {'sheet': 2, 'audio': 1}]

equate.sources.align_sources(sources, *, compare=None, order='monotone', threshold=0.5, window=2)[source]¶

Align N sources of records into groups, one per item, at most one record per source.

sources maps a source name to a sequence of records (dicts, or objects with attributes). The first source is the backbone of the progressive alignment: put the most complete one first.

  • compare: how two records are scored (see record_similarity()): None (shared string fields, ratio), {field: comparator} with equate’s comparator names or any callables, or one record callable (a, b) -> float. Similarities, higher = more alike, roughly in [0, 1].

  • order: 'monotone' (same order, gaps allowed; default), 'free' (optimal assignment), any matcher name from equate.matchers ('greedy', 'stable', …), or a callable taking the gains as a ScoreMatrix (-inf where two records share no compared

    field) and returning (group, record) pairs; pairs without a positive gain are dropped.

  • threshold: two records are aligned only if their similarity is strictly above it. A record joins a group through its best similarity to the group’s members, so one noisy member does not keep it out.

  • window: for 'monotone', how many positions away a swapped item is looked for; for 'monotone' also how many positions away rival records are looked for when computing margin (other orders look everywhere). None: everywhere; 0: no swap repair.

Returns a SourceAlignment (iterate it for SourceGroup items).

A group holds at most one record per source: segmentation is the caller’s job. When one source merges two items into a record (an utterance covering two lines) or splits one over two, split or merge those records before aligning, or the extra one is left as its own group.

With two sources and the items in the same order, this is equate.match() on the shared fields, keeping pairs above threshold.

>>> free_order([[-1, 1], [1, -1]])

equate.sources.monotone_order(gain, *, window=2)[source]¶

Order-preserving pairing of gain’s rows and columns, gaps free.

A Needleman-Wunsch global alignment with gain as substitution score and zero gap cost: the maximum-gain set of non-crossing pairs. Rows and columns it leaves unpaired are then paired by assignment when they have positive gain and lie within window positions of the gap they sit in (a local order swap); those pairs cross the others. window=0 keeps the alignment strictly monotone; None repairs at any distance.

>>> monotone_order([[1, -1], [-1, 1]])
[(0, 0), (1, 1)]
>>> sorted(monotone_order([[-1, 1], [1, -1]]))  # a swap, recovered
[(0, 1), (1, 0)]
>>> monotone_order([[-1, 1], [1, -1]], window=0)
[(0, 1)]
equate.sources.record_similarity(compare=None, *, combine=<function mean>, missing=0.0)[source]¶

Build a record comparator (a, b) -> float from per-field comparators.

compare is:

  • None (default): every field both records hold as a string, scored with DFLT_FIELD_COMPARATOR (two plain strings are compared directly);

  • a mapping {field: comparator} (a registered name or a callable): those fields;

  • a callable (a, b) -> float: returned as it is.

The per-field scores are a comparison_vector() over the fields both records actually have (a field missing or None on either side is left out, not scored zero), reduced by combine: a combiner of equate.compare.vectorize, which takes the {field: score} mapping (the default mean, weighted_sum, fellegi_sunter), or a reducer of numbers (max, min, sum, statistics.median, …), which gets the scores. So a source with extra fields is compared on what it shares. Records sharing no compared field score missing.

>>> sim = record_similarity({'fr': 'ratio', 'zh': 'ratio'}, combine=max)
>>> sim({'fr': 'merci', 'zh': '谢谢'}, {'fr': 'merci', 'zh': '鞋'})  # the better field
1.0

>>> sim = record_similarity({'text': 'ratio'})
>>> sim({'text': 'merci'}, {'text': 'merci', 'pinyin': 'xiexie'})
1.0
>>> sim({'start': 1.0}, {'text': 'merci'})
0.0
